Copilot implementation · Automation system

Moving a writer's attention onto the work only a writer can do.

Writers were spending their best hours on transcription and formatting, the lowest-judgment work in the job, and output quality varied run to run. I separated content generation from formatting, added QA gates and audit logs, and layered SME flagging on top so verification effort could be aimed. Drafting throughput reached 2.3× baseline as a measurable outcome of that redesign.

Role
Knowledge Management Analyst. Owned the pipeline design end to end.
System
Two Copilot agents, KM Drafter and KM Format Editor, with a human review checkpoint between them.
Design intent
Change what a writer spends attention on. Throughput is the outcome, not the goal that shaped the design.
Measured outcome
2.3× drafting throughput against baseline. Drafting stage only.

The challenge

The writing job had the wrong shape.

A writer's scarcest asset is judgment: Deciding what a reader needs, what the source actually says, and what is missing. That is not where the hours were going. Transcription, restructuring, and formatting consumed the best part of the day, and because the output depended on individual discipline rather than on the system, quality varied run to run. Downstream, reviewers read every draft at the same level of suspicion, and nothing recorded which stage produced a defect.

Best hours on the lowest-judgment work

Writers spent their sharpest time transcribing source material and fixing formatting. Every draft started from a blank page.

Inconsistent output quality

Content and formatting varied between runs, so consistency depended on individual discipline rather than on the system.

Review with no signal

Nothing marked which passages were uncertain, so reviewers carried a whole document at one level of suspicion. That is slow and mentally expensive on both sides.

No traceability

When output was wrong, nothing recorded which stage or which inputs produced it. Fixes were guesses.

Strategic approach

Take the non-deterministic work off the model.

The core decision was to stop asking a generative model to do work that did not require judgment. Formatting is deterministic. Validation is deterministic. Only the content itself needs a model, and only accuracy needs a human. Separating those three responsibilities is what gave the writer their judgment hours back.

1

Separate generation from formatting

Formatting moved out of the model and into a deterministic layer, splitting the work across two agents, KM Drafter and KM Format Editor, with a human review checkpoint between them. The model became responsible for content only, and presentation stopped varying between runs.

2

Insert QA gates between stages

Defined pass criteria at each stage boundary so structural and completeness defects were caught inside the pipeline, before anything reached a human reviewer.

3

Add run modes

Built distinct configurations for drafting, full production, and reprocessing, so a correction did not require rerunning the entire pipeline from the start.

4

Add audit logs

Every output became traceable to the stage and inputs that produced it. Failures became diagnosable instead of anecdotal, which made the QA gates tunable over time.

5

Add SME flagging on top

An added feature rather than the core purpose. Missing, conflicting, or uncertain source information surfaces as an explicit flag instead of being silently invented, so neither the writer nor the reviewer carries a whole document at the same level of suspicion. The writer knows what to escalate, and the reviewer knows where to look.

What was built

Deliverables.

The redesign produced a set of durable artifacts, not a one-time fix. Each one is independently maintainable and can be revised without rebuilding the pipeline.

Architecture

Pipeline design

Stage boundaries, responsibilities, and data contracts for a two-agent pipeline with a human review checkpoint and a deterministic formatting layer.

Quality

QA gate criteria

Explicit pass and fail conditions at each stage boundary, defining what may proceed and what must be reprocessed.

Operations

Run mode specification

Draft, full production, and reprocess configurations with defined inputs, outputs, and appropriate use for each.

Traceability

Audit log schema

Structured record of stage, inputs, and outcome for every run, supporting both defect diagnosis and throughput measurement.

Process

SME flagging convention

Rules for what the draft must mark as missing, conflicting, or uncertain, so verification effort can be aimed at the passages that need it.

Evidence

Drafting throughput baseline

Before and after drafts per week, giving the change a measured result rather than an asserted one.

Result

The outcome, measured.

2.3×

drafting throughput against baseline, four active writers

Why it moved

The gain did not come from making the model faster. It came from changing what a writer spends attention on. Once formatting was deterministic and QA gates caught structural defects, the hours that had gone to transcription and cleanup went to judgment instead, and drafting stopped restarting from a blank page.

The audit logs made the result verifiable. Because every run was recorded, the before and after comparison came from the system's own records rather than from recall.

How it was measured: Drafts per week across a defined window before and after, four active writers. The scope is the drafting and authoring stage only. SME review and Salesforce staging are excluded, because their timing is not consistently recorded. The number is the measurable outcome of the redesign, not the goal that shaped it.

Practice

What this demonstrates.

AI workflow design

Assigning generative and deterministic work to the correct layer, so model variability does not propagate into output.

Designing for the operator

Targeting what the person inside the workflow spends attention on, rather than optimizing a number and assuming the experience follows.

Quality gate design

Defining machine-checkable pass criteria that reduce defect load on human reviewers.

Auditability

Building traceability into an AI system so failures are diagnosable and results are verifiable after the fact.

Measurement design

Choosing an outcome metric that cannot be gamed by producing more unfinished work.

Process change

Adding a flagging convention so specialist attention lands on the passages that actually need verification.

Transferable method

The approach generalizes to any AI-assisted production process with a human review step. Start from what the people inside the process spend their attention on, separate deterministic work from generative work, put automated gates in front of the human, and log every run so the result can be measured rather than claimed. The measurement discipline behind the 2.3× figure is documented separately in the Knowledge Ops and Governance Framework case study.
